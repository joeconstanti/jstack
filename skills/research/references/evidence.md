# Evidence and decision briefs

Use a source ledger when several sources or versions inform the answer:

| Claim | Source + locator | Date/version | What it establishes | Limits |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| State a checkable claim | URL + section, or file + line/page | Relevant observation date | Direct observation or reported result | Coverage, incentives, missing data |

Citations must support the adjacent claim. A repository README establishes what
its author promises; code inspection establishes implementation; a successful
run establishes behavior in that environment. Do not silently promote one into
another. Record inspected commit SHAs when comparing changing repositories.

For conflicting sources, check whether they discuss the same period, version,
definition and population. Prefer direct evidence for the precise question,
not simply the newest or most prestigious source. Keep a contradiction visible
if it affects the decision. Identify which options remain viable under it.

For quantitative comparisons, keep units and denominators explicit. Show how
scores and weights were chosen before optimizing against them. Label subjective
rubrics as judgments; include per-item reasons. Do not treat self-scoring as an
independent evaluation. A missing observation is unknown, not zero or success.

A useful decision brief contains the decision, evidence, alternatives (including
keeping the current approach where relevant), recommendation, tradeoffs and
reversal condition. Do not force that template onto a simple factual question.

Stop collecting when the question is answered with sufficient coverage and
additional sources are unlikely to change the decision. If a critical source
is unavailable, bound the conclusion to what the readable evidence supports.
